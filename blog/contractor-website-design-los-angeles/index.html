Contractor Website Design in Los Angeles: What It Costs and What to Look For

July 21, 2026 6 min read Studio-Serra

If you are searching for contractor website design in Los Angeles, you are already ahead of most contractors in your trade. You know the website matters. What is harder to figure out is what it should actually cost, what separates a site that brings in jobs from one that just sits there, and how to tell a good builder from a bad one before you sign anything. Here is a straight answer.

What contractor website design costs in Los Angeles

Pricing in the LA market spreads out fast. Traditional web agencies charge $2,000 to $10,000 for a custom contractor site, plus ongoing fees for hosting and edits. Freelancers on marketplaces often quote $500 to $1,500, but the result is frequently a template with your logo dropped in, no local SEO, and nobody to call when something breaks. DIY builders like Wix or Squarespace run $20 to $50 a month, but you are doing the setup yourself and competing against every other contractor using the same generic template.

What the price difference actually buys

The average homeowner project in Los Angeles runs $3,000 to $15,000. A cheap site that never ranks costs you nothing on paper and every job that found a competitor instead. The price you pay for a website matters far less than whether the website actually shows up when someone searches for what you do.

What separates a site that gets jobs from one that doesn't

Plenty of contractor websites exist purely as a digital business card. They look fine, sit online, and generate nothing. A site built to actually win jobs needs a few specific things in place, and it is worth checking for these before you hire anyone:

  • Local SEO baked in — your services and service area written into the page content, not just listed in a menu
  • A fast, mobile-first build — most homeowners search and click from a phone, often at night
  • Real project photos — before and after shots, not stock images of generic tools
  • A phone number and quote request above the fold — no scrolling required to contact you
  • Service area pages named by city — "roofer Pasadena" ranks differently than "roofer near me"

Questions to ask before you hire a designer

A quick conversation before you sign tells you a lot about what you are actually buying:

  • Is local SEO included, or is it a separate add-on later?
  • Who owns the domain and the site files after launch, you or the agency?
  • What does the monthly fee cover, and what happens if you want to cancel?
  • Can they show other contractor websites they have actually built?
